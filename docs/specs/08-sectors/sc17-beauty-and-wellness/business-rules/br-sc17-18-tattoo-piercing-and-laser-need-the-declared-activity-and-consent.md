# 📏 BR-SC17-18 Tattoo, piercing and laser need the declared activity and consent

**Status:** ✅ specified

## Statement

A tattoo, a piercing or a laser treatment is done only when the salon has declared that activity, the practitioner's
training is valid and the client has signed an informed consent (for a minor, the guardian); a treatment without them is
blocked, and the batches used and the aftercare are recorded.

## Why

These treatments carry health and legal risks for the client and for the salon. The owner asked for regulated care
records ([D561](../../../06-decisions/sectors/d561-benchmark-gaps-sc17-beauty-and-wellness.md)). The France-pack rule
[CR-FR-257 Tattoo, piercing and laser declarations](../../../10-country-packs/france/health-practice/cr-fr-257-tattoo-and-piercing-declaration.md)
holds the law read: the activity declared to the ARS before it starts (CSP R1311-2), the practitioner's hygiene training
(R1311-3), the written consent of a parent or guardian for a minor, proof kept 3 years (R1311-11), and laser or
pulsed-light hair removal only by doctors, nurses and trained qualified beauticians (D1151-2, D1151-3). A tattoo ink carries
on its label a reference that identifies its batch uniquely, and before using it the tattooist gives the client the
information on its packaging or instructions (Regulation (EU) 2020/2081, REACH annex XVII entry 75); the ink's batch is
recorded from that label. No batch rule applies to needles, which are single-use and sterile (R1311-4): their batches
are recorded as a product choice.

## Examples

| Case | Result |
|---|---|
| Activity declared, training valid, consent signed | the treatment may start |
| Activity not declared | blocked |
| Training expired | blocked |
| Minor with the guardian's signature | allowed within the age limit |
| Recalled needle batch | refused |
