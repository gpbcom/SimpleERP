# 🧩 Problem

**Status:** ✅ specified

## The problem

Small and medium companies run on a pile of tools that do not share their data, and on ERPs that are too heavy for them.
Four problems, one answer: one tool for everything, with AI doing the job
([D371](../../06-decisions/vision/d371-vision-line-and-main-promise.md)).

- 🧩 **Tool sprawl:** an ERP, a meeting assistant, a social media tool, a helpdesk and its AI agents, a legal service,
  a website builder, a competitive-intelligence service, each paid separately, each holding part of the company's
  data.
- 🏋️ **ERPs too heavy:** the leading ERPs need an integrator, charge per user and per app, and take months to learn.
- 🛡️ **Data sovereignty:** AI tools send meetings, customers and accounts to clouds the company does not control.
- 🔩 **AI bolted on:** classic ERPs add AI as a paid extra; AI-native ERPs are SaaS only, finance only and seldom French.

## Who has it

Every target, whatever its sector ([D372](../../06-decisions/vision/d372-problem-and-targets.md)):

- 🏪 small companies of 1 to 50 people, with no IT staff, run by the owner and an outside accountant;
- 🏢 SMEs of 50 to 250 people, with some IT staff and several departments;
- 🖥️ IT providers hosting several client companies on one platform (D1);
- 📒 accounting firms running it for their clients.

## Today's alternatives

Four kinds of tools, each solving part of the problem. Named and compared in the
[⚖️ Benchmark](../benchmark/benchmark.md):

- 🎙️ AI meeting assistants: transcripts and summaries, outside the company's records;
- 🎧 AI support-agent platforms: agents working in the company's back office through logins and fixed procedures;
- 🏢 classic ERPs, open source or not: broad, but heavy to set up, priced per user and per app, with AI paid on top;
- 🧠 AI-native ERPs: agents at the core, but SaaS only, mostly finance only, priced on quote, seldom localised for
  France.
