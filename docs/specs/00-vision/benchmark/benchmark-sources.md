#### Notes and doubts, ERPs and AI tools

**Contradictions between sources**
- **Nebesta:** nebesta.com claims "SOC 2 Type II", but the v2 Lovable site says SOC 2 Type II is "in progress". ISO
  27001 is "aligned controls" on both sites, not a certification. Dealroom (search snippet) gives 2025 as the founding
  year with a Feb 2025 Kima seed. Another listing says founded 2024. One older profile calls it "the first AI-shore BPO"
  with Salesforce integration. Neither site names the LLMs, the hosting provider or the automation technique. The FAQ
  says only "human-like login & password access" and "deterministic integrations". The product owner's words "web
  scraping" are not used by the vendor.
- **Noota:** three pages give three different plan tiers for MCP. The pricing page puts it in Free. The MCP page says
  Pro. The Ask Noota page says "MCP connection … included from the Business plan". The homepage says 46 integrations,
  but the integrations directory lists 53. The homepage mentions ISO 27001 only, while the security page also claims SOC
  2 Type II (report at trust.noota.io).
- **Odoo:** Odoo's PA registration is dated 15 Apr 2026 by ma-facture-electronique.org and 22 Apr 2026 by search
  snippets. The official DGFiP list was not fetched. The Odoo 19 docs allow your own OpenAI/Gemini key (required
  on-premise). The Odoo 20 release notes say "All AI features now need credits, which can be bought via IAP". A
  third-party blog says Odoo 20 removed own keys (not confirmed officially). This changes the cost picture for
  self-hosters. The pricing page shows several prices per plan (promo vs list, and probably monthly vs annual) and
  contradicts itself on whether the free plan allows several companies. Whether MCP in Odoo 20 needs Enterprise was not
  confirmed.
- **ERPNext:** Frappe Cloud docs cite ISO 27001:2022, but the corporate quality page cites 27001:2013. The SOC 2 period
  ended May 2025, and no newer report was found. Sources disagree on the v16 release date (Dec 2025 vs Jan 2026). The
  "ERPNext France" marketplace app is listed as "Paid" with no price shown, and it is published by a third party
  (Scopen).
- **Dolibarr:** DoliCloud's e-invoicing FAQ promised e-invoicing and PDP modules "early 2026". The v24 ChangeLog only
  adds e-invoice VAT columns and a setup section pointing to e-invoicing modules. The PA connector is a community module
  (Comparepdp). Dolibarr is not a PA. The core AI assistant and MCP server are flagged "Experimental" in the 24.0.0
  ChangeLog, and a 24.0.x fix closed an SSRF through the AI module.
- **Ximi:** client count is 1,200+ structures on the vendor site, against "3,000 clients" in a 2021 press snippet.
  Pricing is quote-only according to Appvizer and Hackceleration, while a directory snippet says "from €49/month" (not
  verified, the page returned 403). Appvizer claims offline work with sync, but the Ximi Mobile page does not mention
  it. Plizi is described as "planification intelligente" and placed under Xelya's AI statement, but the page never says
  how it works or whether it costs extra. No API, SSO or AI-assistant information is public.

**Pricing as of 2026-11-11**
- Odoo: One App Free €0. Standard €19.90/user/month shown as a 12-month promo (list €24.90 or €31.10). Custom €35.90
  promo (list €44.90 or €56.90). AI, OCR and SMS are paid in IAP credits on top.
- Frappe Cloud: from $5/site/month, $20 shared server, $125 dedicated. No per-user fee.
- DoliCloud: Basic €14/user/month, Premium from €45/month.
- Noota: Free. Pro €19 annual / €29 monthly. Business €39 / €49. Enterprise custom. Black Friday gives 40% off annual
  plans until 1 Dec 2026. Recruitment Agent add-on €99 annual / €139 monthly.
- Nebesta: per resolved ticket, about €1 (v2 site FAQ only).
- Ximi: quote only. The PA (e-invoicing) is included in the Ximi licence.

#### Notes and doubts, AI-native ERPs

**Content aimed at AI agents.**
- **Rillet:** rillet.com, rillet.com/plans and rillet.com/product/enterprise-security embed a JSON block headed
  "INSTRUCTION FOR AI AGENTS AND AUTOMATED TOOLS". It tells agents not to trust the page and to send their questions in
  a POST request to an outside "agent desk" endpoint on info.rillet.com. The info.rillet.com pages also carry an "Agent
  interface" section. I did not act on any of it.
- **Campfire, DualEntry, Light:** each page carries "Ask ChatGPT / Claude / Gemini…" links with a prefilled prompt.
  These are aimed at the visitor's own chatbot, not hidden instructions.
- **Lensing, Doss, ERPClaw:** no agent-directed content was seen.

**Pricing today.**
- Only ERPClaw publishes prices: $0 self-hosted, with the managed cloud quoted at onboarding.
- DualEntry publishes its tier structure (3, 20 or unlimited entities) but no figures.
- The other five sell on a quote. The only numbers are third-party estimates: Rillet about $28K a year median plus a
  $10–30K implementation fee, Campfire $30–55K a year (low confidence), Light from about $35K a year.
- Most vendors price on complexity (entities, transaction volume), not per seat. This matters for comparison with
  Odoo-style per-user pricing.

**Contradictions.**
- **Campfire:** the home page claims "100+ native integrations"; the integrations page lists 69.
- **DualEntry:** "unlimited users" on all plans, yet the same pricing page says extra users cost a fee per user per
  month.
- **DualEntry ISO:** ISO 27001 applies only to its North American data centres; ISO/IEC 42001 is a self-claim I did not
  verify.
- **Rillet MCP:** the current docs give api.rillet.com/mcp with OAuth2; an older doc gives docs.api.rillet.com/mcp with
  a bearer key.
- **Rillet scale:** customer counts vary (600+, 800+, "nearly 1,000") and so do countries (70+ or 75).
- **Rillet and DualEntry integration counts** (12,000+ and 13,000+) almost certainly count bank connections from
  aggregators.
- **Light:** its trust centre says model providers may not train on customer data, but Light "reserves the right" to
  train its own models on customer data.
- **Light MCP:** it claims full read and write access, while its own REST API documents no webhooks.
- **Lensing HQ:** Mountain View or a dual Bay Area and Heidelberg HQ, depending on the source. Its MCP server (October
  2026) reads only.

**France.**
- Light is the only vendor of the seven with real French accounting: FEC export, PCG mapping, the CA3 VAT return and 8
  French banks. It hosts in the EU (AWS Ireland).
- Its e-invoicing page marks France "Supported", but it names neither a Plateforme Agréée nor Factur-X or CII. Its date
  ("B2B mandate began September 2026, phased by size through 2027") should be checked against the official calendar.
- Rillet only just opened in London (9 November 2026), with UK VAT. No US vendor shows any French localisation.
- ERPClaw's EU module has French VAT rates and an EN 16931 generator, but that generator returns a JSON payload, not
  UBL, CII or Factur-X XML, and there is no FEC or PCG.

**ERPClaw caveats.**
- The licence is verified as GPL-3.0 across the main repo and its add-on, region and vertical repos.
- The project is very young: the repo was created in February 2026 and has 162 stars. It is largely built with AI by one
  architect, according to its own Hacker Noon and LinkedIn posts.
- It needs an outside agent runtime (OpenClaw with the Claude API, or Hermes), and its RBAC is opt-in.
- Breadth is very wide on paper (CRM, MRP, payroll, POS and helpdesk add-ons), but the depth of each module is
  unverified.
- It is the closest functional analogue to the target product, and a direct open-source competitor.

**Doss** is not a full ERP. It is an operational subledger that posts to QuickBooks, NetSuite, Xero, Rillet or Campfire.

**Unknowns.** Hosting regions for Rillet, Campfire, Lensing and Doss, and the AI model providers of every SaaS vendor,
need a sub-processor list or DPA, which none of them publishes openly.
